# Idea brief

## Origin story
Founder wanted a fishing rod and reel. Spent ~20 minutes browsing Facebook Marketplace to find a candidate, then 50–100 minutes across Chrome and retailers (BCF and others) checking what it costs new, before feeling safe to commit. The work was: find candidates, find the new price, judge whether the used price is actually a good deal.

## Problem
Shoppers want the best deal, but supply is fragmented across new retail (retailer sites, Amazon, eBay) and pre-owned channels (Facebook Marketplace, Gumtree, eBay, pawn/resale stores). No single place answers "new vs used, local vs shipped — what's the best deal and is it worth it?" The shopper does the cross-referencing by hand.

## Vision
An app on the App Store and Google Play where the user enters an item and chooses new, pre-owned or both. The app searches across the internet, finds the best deals in each category, and helps the user decide whether to buy.

## Required capabilities (from the founder)
- Search by item; filter new / used / combined
- Location-based options (local pickup vs shipped)
- Best-deal identification across sources
- A buy / don't-buy decision aid, not just a list of prices
- Memorable, go-to product — not a commodity comparison table

## Context
- Home market: Australia (BCF, Facebook Marketplace). Global expansion is a later question.
- Goal is a commercial product, not an academic report. Thesis-style rigour, practical output.

## What the research must settle
1. Has this already been done? By whom, how well, and why did earlier attempts succeed or fail?
2. Is it feasible — technically, legally, and economically?
3. What is the real gap, and what would make this product different enough to be remembered?
4. What methodology gets us from here to a validated, buildable product?
