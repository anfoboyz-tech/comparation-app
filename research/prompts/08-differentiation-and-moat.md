# Track 8 — Differentiation, product concepts and defensibility

Output: `research/findings/08-differentiation-and-moat.md`

## Goal
The founder wants something people remember and return to, not another comparison table. This track looks outward for the patterns that made consumer decision products memorable and defensible, and for what general AI assistants structurally cannot do, then proposes concrete product concepts.

## Questions
1. **What made category-defining decision products work?** Study the mechanism, not the hype, for: Carsales/RedBook and CarGurus (deal ratings), Kayak and Hopper (buy/wait prediction — and how accurate Hopper's predictions were shown to be), Skyscanner, Zillow Zestimate (including its failure modes), Honey, Camelcamelcamel and Keepa, OzBargain, Trolley and Frugl (grocery), GasBuddy and PetrolSpy, Shazam (one-tap utility), Vivino (scan a label, get a verdict), Yuka (scan a product, get a score). What single interaction made each one memorable, and what data or network asset made each one hard to copy?
2. **The general-assistant threat.** In November 2026, what can ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Meta AI and Amazon's assistant already do when asked "is this Marketplace listing a good deal" or "find me the best deal new or used on X in Brisbane"? Where do they fall down (local second-hand listings, logged-in marketplaces, sold-price history, location, alerts over time, accountability for accuracy)? Which of those weaknesses are structural and which will disappear within a year? A product that a general assistant will absorb in twelve months is not worth building, so be hard-nosed.
3. **Defensible assets.** What could this product accumulate that a larger company could not copy quickly: a proprietary dataset of observed used-item asking and outcome prices by region, user-contributed listing data, a verdict accuracy track record, community, category depth, trust and neutrality positioning, seller-side relationships? Rate each on how achievable it is for a small team and how strong the resulting moat would be.
4. **Product concepts.** Propose four to six distinct concepts that fit the founder's vision, each with: the one-sentence promise, the core interaction, what data it needs, what makes it memorable, the moat, the main risk, and how hard it is to build. Include at least: (a) the founder's original "search everything" framing, (b) a "check this listing" verdict tool entered through the share sheet, (c) a "cheapest way to own X" total-cost ranking across new, used, refurbished and local, (d) a watch-and-alert agent that hunts over days, and (e) one concept of your own that none of the briefs anticipated.
5. **Naming and positioning signals.** What positioning is unclaimed in Australia (for example "the RedBook for everything", "a fair-price check for anything second-hand")? Do not spend effort on brand names; identify the position.
6. **Recommendation.** Which concept, or combination, would you back, and what evidence would change your mind?
