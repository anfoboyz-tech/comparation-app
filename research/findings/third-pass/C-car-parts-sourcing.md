# Third pass, Assignment C — the car-parts sourcing problem in Australia

Date read: 17 November 2026. 25 of 25 searches used.

**How to read the labels.** [S] = I read the page itself. [SS] = search-result summary only, page not read. [B] = my background knowledge, not checked in this session. [I] = my inference. Confidence is given on the claims that matter.

## Bottom line

1. The pain is real and the industry says so itself. eBay Australia says only about 20% of recycled-parts sales happen online [S]; a trade start-up's press release says 90% of used parts are still sourced by phone and that a wrecker has the requested part about 30% of the time [S, the company's own unsourced claims]; an OzBargain commenter who rang more than a dozen yards reports that half do not answer the phone [S, one person, 2017].
2. "One request, many wreckers" already exists in Australia four times over, free to the buyer: Findapart (since 2000), Hollander Parts (since 2018, "1 Million+" parts of live stock), WrecKonnect (2025, for mechanics) and PartsClub (100+ wreckers, quotes "usually within 1 hour"). A request form is not the gap.
3. What I did not find anywhere in Australia is a single agent that checks the online stock, the request networks, eBay, the new-part price and the phone-only yards for one part, sorts out fitment, and chases the yards that do not reply. Not finding it is not proof it does not exist.
4. "Five minutes" is achievable only for the stock that is already listed online. For the phone-only yards the honest promise is "five minutes of your time, answers over the next few hours". Existing services quote "within hours" for the same reason: a person at the yard has to go and look.
5. An agent phoning or emailing wreckers to ask to buy a part looks lawful in Australia on my reading, because an enquiry to buy is not telemarketing or a commercial message. The moment the message also promotes the platform to the wrecker, it is. Oneflare paid a A$75,600 penalty for texting businesses whose numbers it took from public directories [S]. This needs a lawyer before any outbound contact at volume, and I could not read ACMA's own pages.
6. Google is the main threat to the generic part of the idea. Its AI already phones US businesses for prices and stock, started with auto shops, and discloses itself as an AI on a recorded line [S via a trade write-up]. It is US-only today.
7. Car parts is a better first market for a sourcing agent than general second-hand goods: the suppliers are businesses that want enquiries, no Marketplace access is needed, the item has a hard identifier, and the industry is A$1.7bn with no dominant player [SS]. It is still a hard business: a consumer needs it rarely, three of the four incumbents charge the buyer nothing, and a 26-year-old free service has not become a large company.
8. Cheapest next step: put the founder's actual drive shaft, and about 20 other real requests, through PartsClub, Findapart and Hollander Parts and time them. If they return a usable quote within a few hours most of the time, the gap is that nobody knows they exist, which is a marketing problem and not an agent problem.

Confidence that the search commonly takes hours for an uncommon part: medium. Reddit and Whirlpool returned nothing in search, so this section rests on one forum thread and on companies describing the problem they sell against.

### 3. Where the inventory lives

- **Yard systems.** Hollander's Pinnacle Professional (Solera group) is the named Australian system; Hollander Parts stock comes only from "approved vehicle dismantlers across Australia that used Hollander International's Pinnacle Professional Software" [S]. InfraByte is a cloud system listing Australia as a market [SS]. Many small yards have no system (the OzBargain account above).
- **Searchable online.** Hollander Parts (live stock), PartsClub (listed stock plus eBay sync), eBay itself, and individual wreckers' own sites. No public API was found for any of them. eBay's API is for approved partners only (second-pass finding), and Assignment A covers eBay's 2026 ban on LLM-driven bots.
- **How much is phone-only.** By sales, about 80% is offline on eBay's figure [S]. By business count: about 150 recyclers were on Hollander Parts at launch in 2018 [S], against about 1,665 dismantling businesses in 2026 [SS], which is roughly 9% [I]. Even inside the computerised yards, only 1.5m of 4–6m parts met the listing standard; the rest was reachable only by a "part request" [S]. My reading: most yards by number are discoverable only by phone or a visit, while late-model, higher-value stock is concentrated in the computerised minority. Confidence: medium on the direction, low on any percentage.
- **Industry bodies and standards.** The Auto Parts Recyclers Association of Australia was the peak body, with about 330 members in 2008 [SS]; an "Auto Recyclers Association of Australia" is quoted as saying legitimate wreckers are falling about 10% a year [SS, page blocked]. eBay says it is working with the Motor Traders Association of Australia and state associations on "a common framework for how parts are dismantled, tested and listed" [S], which tells you no data standard exists yet.

### 4. Fitment data

A wrecker matches a part by make, model, year, body, engine code and side, then by OEM part number or an interchange code. The OzBargain thread shows the exchange: "alternator for a NM Pajero" / "3 litre or 3.5?" / "3.5, it's the 6G74 engine" [S].

| Source | What it is | Access and cost |
|---|---|---|
| Hollander Interchange | The recycler-side interchange standard; Hollander Parts shows the interchange line under each part title [S] | Licensed inside Hollander's products; prices unpublished [SS] |
| Partly (NZ) | Fitment data, now an AI model ("Interpreter") for repairers. Raised US$50m Series B at a US$500m valuation; claims 98.8% parts-list accuracy against 92.1% for existing tools, its own benchmark [S, iTWire] | Enterprise; has an API; pricing not found |
| PARts DB (partsdb.com.au) | Plate and VIN decoding "delivered as an API or web service" [SS] | By subscription; price not found |
| Autoinfo OSCAR | Aftermarket catalogue with rego lookup [SS] | Trade subscription |
| Infomedia | VIN API in front of manufacturer parts catalogues [SS] | Dealer-oriented |
| JATO, S&P Global Mobility | Rego and VIN mapping. S&P argues rego lookups "often lack the vehicle-specific information needed for correct fitment" [SS] | Enterprise |
| NEVDIS (the national registration database) | Rego to VIN and vehicle description, through a contracted gateway named in a forum reply as Blue Flag [SS] | A reseller lists A$0.30 per lookup in blocks of 100 [SS, unconfirmed] |

No licence price was published by any provider. A first version does not need to own fitment data: it needs to collect what the wrecker will ask for (rego or VIN, engine code, the number stamped on the old part, a photo) and let the yard confirm interchange in its own system [I].

### 5. An agent that makes the enquiries

**What exists.**

| Product | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Google "Ask for Me", later "Have AI check prices" | AI phones local businesses for price and availability and summarises. First tested with auto shops and nail salons; later widened to home services. Businesses can opt out | US only [SS] |
| Gemini "Call for Me" | Phones a business from the user's own number for stock, quotes and bookings. Opens by "disclosing that it is an AI assistant from Google calling on a recorded line on your behalf"; cannot pay; cannot be used for telemarketing; daily call limits | Pixel 11, US, paid plan [S, Local Falcon, 9 Oct 2026] |
| Parrot (Y Combinator) | "AI coworker for collision repair shops", including parts procurement [S]; a directory says it calls multiple suppliers [SS] | US |
| PartsNow.ai | Truck-parts sourcing by chat, voice and photo [SS] | US |
| Yard-side AI (Brilo and others) | AI answers the wrecker's phone or WhatsApp and quotes from stock [SS] | US and elsewhere |

Nothing was found in Australia that makes enquiries on a buyer's behalf. Confidence: medium.

**Australian rules.**

| Rule | What I could establish | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Do Not Call Register Act 2006 | The regulator's page defines a telemarketing call as one made "to offer, supply, provide, advertise or solicit" goods or services [S]. A call asking whether a yard has a part and at what price is a request to buy, so on my reading it falls outside [I]. The register is for private and domestic numbers [B] | Medium; lawyer to confirm |
| Spam Act 2003 (email, SMS) | The same logic applies to an enquiry [I, Act text not readable]. If the message also invites the wrecker to join or pay for a platform, it is commercial. Consent can be inferred from a published business address only where the message relates to the recipient's line of work [SS]. Oneflare paid A$75,600 for "sending commercial SMS messages to phone numbers it found on public directories" and without an unsubscribe [S, iTWire] | Medium |
| Call recording | State law. Hamilton Locke: lawful for a participant to record without consent in Queensland, Victoria and the Northern Territory; other states are stricter; the federal interception Act is aimed at third parties [S]. Other summaries conflict [SS] | Low; treat as all-party consent everywhere and announce recording |
| AI disclosure | No Australian rule requiring an AI caller to identify itself was found in a primary source. Vendor blogs say ACMA expects it under misleading-conduct law [SS]. Google's practice is to disclose in the first sentence | Low on the law; disclose anyway |
| Privacy | Wreckers' staff voices and the buyer's rego and phone number are personal information [I] | Not researched |

ACMA's website failed to load three times, and AustLII and the legislation register blocked or did not render, so none of this was read in the Act.

**Practical limits [I].** Half the yards do not answer; the person answering has to walk the yard; small yards quote from memory and forget (the OzBargain story of the owner who forgot his own quote); and at a 30% hit rate, seven calls in ten waste the wrecker's time, in an industry already "receiving phone calls all day for parts they don't have". An agent that phones every yard for every request will get blocked. It should check listed stock first and phone only the yards likely to have that make.

### 6. Market and money

- **Industry.** Motor vehicle dismantling and used parts wholesaling: A$1.7bn revenue in 2025–26, about 1,665 businesses, no company above 5% share [SS, IBISWorld; pages show 1,633 and 1,665 for different years]. The wider parts aftermarket is put at US$10.5bn by one research house [SS, low confidence].
- **Demand side.** eBay's YouGov survey: 82% of vehicle owners open to recycled parts, about one in five drivers do some of their own repairs [S, eBay's claims]. Capricorn, the repairers' co-operative, has more than 19,000 members across Australasia [SS].
- **Part values.** Listings on Hollander Parts today: door lock $85, steering rack $80–$240, alloy wheel $165, mirror $550, engine $2,550, gearbox $3,950 [S]. WrecKonnect: used is on average under half the new price [S, its claim].
- **Who pays, on what was found.**

| Payer | Evidence | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer, per search | None found; every locator is free to buyers | Needed perhaps once every year or two [I] |
| Mechanic or panel shop, subscription | WrecKonnect, PartsCheck (founded 2010, collision repair quoting [S]) and Parrot all target the trade | Incumbents with integrations; a different product from a consumer app |
| Wrecker, lead fee or listing | The UK "advertising platform" model | PartsClub advertises "no commissions"; Findapart is free to wreckers; the Oneflare penalty shows the legal risk in pushing leads at businesses |
| Affiliate on the sale | PartsClub discloses eBay commission [S]; Supercheap Auto is on Commission Factory (second pass) | Cash sales at the counter pay nothing |

Rough arithmetic [I, unverified inputs]: a part at A$200 with a 5% commission earns A$10, against A$0.06 per check in the earlier product. Fifteen two-minute AI calls might cost A$3–6. The margin is positive only if the sale can be tracked, which a cash pickup defeats.

### 8. Other categories with the same shape

Search found no evidence on these, so this ranking is my judgement only. Confidence: low.

1. **Truck, farm and earthmoving machinery parts.** Highest pain because downtime costs money, the buyer is a business that will pay, and supply is dismantlers and dealers reached by phone. Findapart already lists these categories; PartsNow.ai is doing it for trucks in the US.
2. **Motorcycle, 4WD, caravan and marine parts.** Same suppliers and same method as cars, so nearly free to add.
3. **Discontinued appliance and whitegoods parts.** Fragmented and partly offline, easy to reach by email, but low value per part.

Tyres are already well indexed online. Trades quotes are crowded (hipages, Oneflare, Airtasker). Building materials are a business-to-business quoting problem with its own incumbents.

### 9. Verdict

**Yes, car parts is a better first market for a sourcing agent than second-hand general goods**, with medium confidence.

| Test | Second-hand general goods | Used car parts |
|---|---|---|
| Can the supply be reached lawfully? | No for Marketplace, doubtful for Gumtree | Yes for phone, email and web forms to businesses, subject to the checks in section 5 |
| Is the pain documented? | Forum posters already had their answer within the hour | The industry and its start-ups describe hours of phoning |
| Is there a hard identifier? | Rarely | Rego, VIN, part number |
| Is it already built? | A dozen verdict tools, no users | Four request services, traction unknown |
| Money per use | About A$0.06 | Perhaps A$10 if the sale is tracked |
| How often is it needed? | Often | Rarely for a consumer; weekly for a mechanic |

**What a first version would do.**
1. Take rego and state, the part, a photo of the old part or its number, postcode and how far the buyer will travel.
2. Ask the two or three fitment questions a wrecker would ask.
3. Within minutes, show what is already listed (by partnership, affiliate link or approved API, not by scraping) beside the new price at an aftermarket retailer and the dealer.
4. Over the next hours, send one AI-disclosed enquiry to a short list of yards that wreck that make, by the channel each yard prefers, and chase once.
5. Return one table: price, condition, kilometres, warranty, freight, distance, and whether used is worth it against new. That last column is where the earlier fair-price work carries over.

**What would stop it.**
- The existing free services already answer most requests within hours. Untested, and the first thing to test.
- Wreckers do not answer, hang up on an AI, or block a number that calls too often.
- A wrong-fitment part and the return that follows land on the product's reputation.
- A consumer uses it once and has no reason to come back.
- No trackable sale, so no revenue.
- Google launches AI calling in Australia, or PartsClub or Hollander adds the same agent on top of data they already hold.
- A lawyer reads the outbound messages as commercial.

## What this means for the product

- Do not build another request form. Four exist.
- The defensible work is the part the incumbents skip: one answer across listed stock, request networks, phone-only yards and new parts, with fitment questions asked up front and non-responders chased.
- Sell the promise honestly: minutes of the user's time, hours of elapsed time.
- Decide early between consumer and mechanic. The consumer story is the founder's; the repeat use and the willingness to pay are with the mechanic, where WrecKonnect, PartsCheck and Parrot already aim.
- Approach PartsClub and Hollander Parts as partners before treating them as data sources. They hold the stock and the wrecker relationships.
- Keep every outbound message a pure enquiry. No sign-up invitation, no platform pitch, AI and recording disclosed in the first sentence.
